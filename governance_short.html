Decisions and Governance

The idea

I trust your judgment, and I'm comfortable with you leading as CEO. I don't want every decision to need mutual approval. But with a large part of my wealth tied to the business for many years, I wouldn't be comfortable having no say in fundamental decisions.

My idea is simple: each of us has the freedom to act without needing the other's approval, within boundaries and ranges we agree on up front. We set the strategy together, and mutual agreement is needed only for what falls outside those boundaries. Of course, we will discuss things with each other, but each of us has the final say in our own areas of responsibility.

What we agree on up front

I believe this approach can work because we're not a startup looking for a business model, which might require pivots. We're entering growth mode and need to execute a plan: finance, supplier discovery, logistics, and automating and tracking every part of the business. The current software can already source $1 billion and reprice $100 million of products without substantial infrastructure changes, so I see almost no risk there.

I'd propose:

  • Focus: Amazon branded resale, probably expanding to Walmart and channels fulfilled by Amazon.
  • Technology at the core: algorithms decide what we buy and sell, with exceptions we agree on.
  • Goal: $100M to $200M of working capital in five years. This also sets the bar for how much risk we take.
  • Growth: organic (new suppliers, buying more from existing ones) or through acquisitions.
  • Financing: debt or investors. My preference is debt: a third party can change the dynamics of how the two of us run the company.

Who decides what

A short list of major decisions needs both of us. Everything else falls in one of our areas, where each of us has the final say.

Each of us also decides how much of our own profit share to take out or leave in the business, within liquidity limits we agree on.
